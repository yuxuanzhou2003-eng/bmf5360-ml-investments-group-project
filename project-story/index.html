From signals to an investable strategy

A plain-language view of our machine-learning equity research project: what we are testing, what the early evidence says, and what must happen before we trust the result.

The honest headlineThe model ranks stocks somewhat better in validation, but the first realistic portfolio still lost money. That gap is the project’s main lesson—not something to hide.
Status: research prototype. Development data have been used; the final 2023–2026 test labels are still sealed for the 49-stock AI pilot.
01 · Idea

Can we turn many weak clues into a better stock ranking?

Instead of guessing whether the whole market rises tomorrow, we ask a narrower question: among AI-industry companies, which stocks are more likely to beat SPY over the next 21 trading days?

Research guardrail

We separate learning from judging

A model may only learn from the past. Validation checks whether it works on later, unseen development years. The final test stays closed until the complete rulebook is frozen.

03 · Baseline model

Start simple, then ask whether complexity helps

Think of the model as a disciplined scorekeeper. It reads only information already known on that day, gives every stock a score, and ranks the list. A higher score means “more likely to beat SPY over the next 21 trading days” - not “guaranteed to rise.”

1. What it readsRecent momentum, volatility, trading activity, market conditions and AI-industry state.
2. A simple exampleIf Stock A scores 0.62 and Stock B 0.48, A ranks above B. This is a ranking, not a guaranteed return.

Why Logistic is the main baseline

Better signal, easier to explain

The AI-state Logistic model improves on technical-only Logistic: AUC 0.5799 vs 0.5395, and mean daily Rank IC 0.0889. Random Forest did not show a consistent gain, so adding complexity is not automatically useful.

04 · Early results

A better ranking did not become a better portfolio - yet

The pre-registered validation portfolio bought high-score names, applied position and industry limits, added a SPY beta hedge, and charged spread costs. It is a useful reality check: model metrics alone are not enough.

The comparison that matters

Same window: SPY +13.11%

Costs were only about 0.24% of initial NAV, so costs do not explain the loss. Four skipped-rebalance windows were particularly damaging. That is a clue for a new hypothesis, not permission to quietly optimize the old validation result.

05 · Next steps

What we should do before claiming success

  1. Choose one final story: keep the 49-stock AI pilot as the main study; present event-network and hierarchical strategies only as separate exploratory work.
  2. Freeze a v2 rulebook before looking at final test results: universe, features, model choice, portfolio mapping, costs, and risk limits.
  3. Run a strict sensitivity check using only the 9 names with direct point-in-time evidence, and clearly label the broader 49-stock universe as exploratory.
  4. Open the sealed test once—then report both predictive metrics and investable portfolio outcomes, including uncertainty and failure cases.
Presentation takeaway

The project is credible because it is careful

Our strongest claim is not “we found alpha.” It is: “we built a leakage-aware research pipeline, found a modest predictive signal, and learned exactly where the signal failed to survive portfolio implementation.”
